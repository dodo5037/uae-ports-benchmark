Gulf container ports, 2020 to 2024 · the answer

Jebel Ali's port-performance score fell 80 points in its busiest year since 2015; the first place to look is ship time before berth

  • What happened. On the World Bank index of ship time in port, Jebel Ali went from 73 to -7 (rank 262 of 403) and Khalifa Port from 105 to 46 (rank 68) between 2023 and 2024.
  • It is not only the Red Sea. The median port lost 2 points. Hamad Port and Sohar lost 3. Singapore handled a record volume and kept a score of 88.

What happened

Both UAE hubs lost ground in 2024: Jebel Ali fell from 73 to -7 and Khalifa Port from 105 to 46

The index measures how long container ships spend in port, compared with ships of the same size and workload. 0 is the 2024 world average; higher is better.

Jebel Ali: rank 262 of 403 in 2024. Khalifa Port: rank 68.

Hamad Port (125) and Salalah (117) stayed in the world's top 15.

All five years are measured against the 2024 baseline, so the lines can be compared across years (report p. 53).

Source: World Bank (2025), CPPI 2020 to 2024, data annex; printed table pp. 63-64. CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO.2
Is it just the Red Sea?

The Red Sea year cost the median port 2 points; Jebel Ali lost 80 and Khalifa Port 59

214 of the 387 ports scored in both years got worse. Only 21 fell further than Jebel Ali.

Neighbours in the same waters barely moved: Hamad Port -3, Sohar -3.

Singapore, also a transshipment hub, handled a record 40 million TEU in 2024 and kept a score of 88, rank 29 (report p. 37).

So the crisis explains a fall, but not one of this size. Dammam (-88) shows the UAE hubs were not alone.

Source: World Bank (2025), CPPI 2020 to 2024, data annex and p. 37. Median and counts are my calculations from the annex.3
Where to look first

A third of ship time at Jebel Ali passes before the ship is tied up, against a fifth at a typical port

Time before berth is waiting at anchor plus sailing in. Khalifa Port 41%, Jebel Ali 33%, median of 403 ports 21%.

Jebel Ali handled 15.5 million TEU against 19.4 million of stated capacity: about 80%, in a year when diverted ships arrived off schedule.

Queues grow fast when a busy system gets irregular arrivals. That is a reason to check berth waiting first.

Caution: this is a ratio. A port that unloads fast also shows a high share: Salalah is at 36% and ranks 15th.

Sources: World Bank (2025), CPPI annex ("Berth hours in % of port hours"); DP World release, 19 Feb 2025. Utilisation is my division of DP World's two figures.4
What to do next

Before choosing between scheduling fixes and new capacity, measure waiting-for-berth per call and test whether it rose with volume

Next steps for a terminal operator

  • Get the per-call data: hours at anchor, hours at berth and arrival against schedule, by week, for 2023 and 2024.
  • Test the queueing explanation: did waiting rise in the weeks with the most volume or the most off-schedule arrivals?
  • If yes, start with scheduling: agreed berthing times shared with carriers, as Singapore used in 2024 (p. 37), and berth windows (p. 35). Capacity comes second.

What this analysis cannot tell you

  • No causes. The index reports time in port, not why (report p. 52).
  • No hours. Scores and one ratio per port per year; nothing per ship or per week.
  • One provider, three UAE ports. Data from S&P Global via the World Bank; Khor Fakkan and Fujairah are not in the index.
