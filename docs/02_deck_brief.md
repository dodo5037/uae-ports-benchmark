# Step 2: your part. Write the 5 slide titles

*About 45 minutes. You write; I coach. Send me the five titles in chat when done.*

## Why titles are the skill

A consulting deck is read by its titles alone. If someone reads only your five titles, top to bottom, they must get the whole argument. This is what Oliver Wyman, PwC and DP World's strategy teams mean by "executive summary", and it is what an interviewer hears when you "walk through a recommendation".

## Four rules

1. **A title is a full sentence that states the conclusion.** Not a label.
2. **It contains a number** from the chart below it (where the slide has a chart).
3. **It claims only what the data supports.** Use "suggests" or "is consistent with" for causes.
4. **Two lines at most** (about 15 words).

Example on a different topic, so I don't write yours for you:
- Label (bad): "Forecast accuracy by method"
- Conclusion (good): "A damped-trend forecast cuts error by 26% against repeating last week's sales"

## Think about these before you write (they are the interview questions)

- Slide 3: Hamad Port lived through the same Red Sea year and lost 3 points. What does that let you say, and what does it not?
- Slide 4: why is "33% of time before berth" a reason to look at berth waiting, and why is it not proof? (Hint: what would that percentage be at a port that unloads very fast?)
- Slide 4: your own fulfilment simulation showed queues exploding at 94% utilisation. How does that idea apply to a port at about 80%, and where does the comparison break down?
- Slide 5: if you were given one more dataset to settle the question, what would you ask for?
